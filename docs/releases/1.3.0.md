# Build Fetcher 1.3.0

**1.3.0 teaches the model League's stats and combat math, and adds a larger team network that waits, dormant,
until enough games exist to train it.**

The installer is per user, with no admin rights. It installs over any earlier version and keeps your settings, key,
games and model in `%APPDATA%\build-fetcher\`. Your current model keeps working and goes on learning from where it is.
It isn't code-signed yet, so Windows SmartScreen warns on first run: choose **More info → Run anyway**.

## What's new
- **The model understands stats, not only item names.**
  - Every item's full stat list comes from Riot's Data Dragon: AD, AP, attack speed, crit, lethality, armor and magic
    penetration, haste, health, resistances, life steal, omnivamp and more. So do each champion's base stats and
    growth per level.
  - For every player at every sampled minute, the model now computes League's combat math:
    - how much of your damage gets through the enemy's armor and magic resist after your penetration;
    - your resistances against their damage mix;
    - crit and on-hit with attack speed, and ability haste on casters;
    - your sustain against their anti-heal, and your anti-heal against their healing;
    - wasted stats, such as magic resist against a team with little magic damage.
  - Each champion and role learns its own taste for each stat, so champions can learn "Irelia wins with attack speed"
    without ability ratios, which Data Dragon doesn't publish.
  - In game, each candidate item is scored with the math recomputed as if you had bought it, against the enemies'
    actual levels and items.
- **The team network (dormant).** A larger network that looks at all ten players at once: champion, items, build
  stats and base stats at their level.
  - It sleeps until **200,000 games with a timeline** exist. The Model page shows the progress, for example "Team
    network: dormant, 12,345 of 200,000 games with a timeline (6%)".
  - Then it wakes up on its own. It learns every new game, works through the stored games in the background, and is
    checked every 150 games with the same strict check.
  - It goes live only once it beats the model in force. It runs on the processor (about 69,000 weights).

## Measured
On Linux, synthetic data:
- 166 Rust tests and 7 web tests pass. New tests cover:
  - item stats parsed from Data Dragon (for example Lord Dominik's 35% armor penetration);
  - the damage formula: Lord Dominik's lets through more against armor-stacked enemies than against squishy ones;
  - the team network's hand-written gradients against finite differences;
  - the team network sleeping, waking up at its threshold, being judged, and staying awake after a restart.
- **Learning one game:** 6.1 ms (5.6 ms in 1.2). A check: 0.8 s on 1,500 held-out matches (0.5 s).
- **Continuous learning still improves the model:** on 1,500 new games, 6 of 10 checks were accepted, and held-out log
  loss went from 0.4965 to 0.4921.
- **The team network's first check** on a 5,000-game test set was rejected (log loss 0.50725 against 0.50718): too
  few games to beat steps 1 and 2. That's why it sleeps until 200,000. The check took 0.6 s.
- **Memory:** the logistic model is 16.5 MiB (15.0 before, +65,536 slots). The team network is 1.6 MiB, plus its
  replay examples (up to about 20 MB) once awake.

## Not verified before release
- Whether the stat features improve the model on your real games. On synthetic data, where no stat effect was planted
  beyond the armor case, they cost a little noise; the safety check decides on your data.
- The team network on real data, and its CPU load once awake. It will only wake up after weeks of fetching.
- How long refilling the counts takes at start-up with a full database (one SQL count, expected to take milliseconds).
- The first model's check on a brand-new install stays at the edge of its calibration bound on some small test sets,
  as before 1.3.0. Your install already has a model, so this doesn't affect you.
