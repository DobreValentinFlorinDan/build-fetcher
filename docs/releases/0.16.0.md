## Quick updates
A full evaluation now saves what it measured next to its report: every held-out game's results, the settings it chose, and hashes of the match and player IDs (never the IDs themselves), about 46 MB at 82,000 games. A **quick update** reads the games, scores only the new ones with those settings, adds them, and decides every verdict again with the same rules. With no new games it gives the full evaluation's report again, number for number.

Measured on your games (82,000, two threads):

| New games added | Quick update | Full evaluation |
|---|---|---|
| 118 | 151 s | 1,039 s |
| 286 | 173 s | 1,203 s |
| 970 | 231 s | 1,931 s |
| none | 68 s | |

A quick update keeps the full evaluation's settings, so it can't follow what a full one would choose anew. On your games, 118 new games of the young patch 16.21 moved a full evaluation's draft-adjusted lift by 0.05 pts and flipped fine-tuning; a quick update stayed within 0.015 pts of the full evaluation it added to. So:

- **While a patch is young, every evaluation stays full**, as in 0.15.2: a full one runs once the current patch has 30 % more games than the last full evaluation saw.
- **Once a patch has many games, quick updates run between full ones.** A full one still runs after a new patch or a region moving to one, 20 % more games, a week, an app or Data Dragon update, or when new games move a verdict clearly across its line. A verdict the new games only move onto its line keeps the full evaluation's (fine-tuning sits there on your games).

The time saved is an estimate, not yet measured over a patch: about half the evaluation time a patch at today's size, more as the games grow.

## Data tab
- **Evaluate builds** runs what fits: a quick update or a full evaluation. The line under it says which, and why ("Next: full evaluation — new patch 16.22").
- **Full evaluation** runs a full one whenever you want. Asked for while a quick update runs, it replaces it.
- **The automatic evaluation** runs quick updates as often as 0.15.2 ran evaluations (300 new games, or 3 % of those the last one saw), at most every hour, and full ones when the plan says so, at most every 6 hours.
- **Reports and the history** say whether each was a quick update or a full evaluation, and why. Quick updates are saved as `eval-update-…` (`eval-auto-update-…` when automatic).

## Also
- Your first evaluation after installing is a full one: a new app version may measure differently.
- `npm run eval:builds` stays full; `--auto` follows the plan and `--update` adds the new games to the last full evaluation.
- Data and policy are unchanged: Riot API and Data Dragon only, nothing leaves your PC, and no new dependencies or schema changes.
