# Irelia Companion 0.9.0

- **Runes against the draft:** runes, summoners, the start and the skill order can now be judged against what the draft predicted, not the overall win rate. It turns on only if Evaluate builds finds it better on your games.
- **A "vs" block in the imported item set:** the start and first items that did best against your lane opponent.
- **Evaluation history:** a chart of every evaluation in the Data tab, good or bad at a glance.
- **Champ select is fast at your size:** after the first draft, each update takes tens of milliseconds instead of 1–3 seconds.

## Runes against the draft
- **The problem:** a rune, summoner spell, start or skill order is chosen before the game, so there is no game state to judge it against. It was judged by the result minus the overall win rate. A choice taken mostly into hard matchups, on a weak patch or by weaker players was blamed for them; one favoured by strong players was credited for them.
- **Now:** each stored game gets an expected win rate before it started, from what was known then: the draft, the lane opponent, the lane, the patch, the region, and the player's rank, LP and Irelia mastery. A choice is credited only with how much more often than expected its games were won. Items are judged against the game state, as before.
- **Grounded in statistics:**
  - The expected win rate is a regularised logistic regression. Each lane opponent's effect is shrunk towards zero, so a matchup with few games can't swing it.
  - The choices themselves are in the fit, then set to their average mix. Without that, a strong player's skill would hide part of what the choices strong players favour actually do.
  - Each game's expected win rate comes from a model that never saw that player's games, so a game's result can't flatter its own baseline.
  - How much to regularise is chosen by cross-validation on your games.
- **Tested:**
  - **Synthetic games** where strong players take Ignite much more often and Ignite costs 8 points: judged by the overall win rate, Ignite looks 6 points *better*; judged against the draft, it costs 8 (−8.1 on average over eight seeds).
  - **Test databases where nothing predicts the result:** the expected win rate is no better than the overall one, so the option stays off.
- **When it turns on:** Evaluate builds compares it with today's scoring on held-out games, judging both by the same yardstick. It turns on only if three things hold:
  - the expected win rate predicts held-out results better than the overall win rate;
  - the gain is above zero with 95 % confidence;
  - its win chances are no less accurate (Brier score).
- **Data tab:** a **Runes against the draft** switch sits next to Patch memory. **Follow the evaluation** now decides both; moving either switch makes the choice yours.
- **Cost:** Evaluate builds takes longer, since it now also tests this: 13.5 min instead of 10.3 on the 54,000-game test database. That is about 8 instead of 6 on your games (an estimate). Everything else in the report is the same to the last digit.

## A "vs" block in the imported item set
- When you save the build to the League client in champ select, the item set gets two extra blocks: the start that did best against your lane opponent, right after the build's start, and the first items that did best (up to two), right after the core. Each is titled with the opponent and the games behind it, e.g. "vs Darius: best start (24 games)".
- "Did best" is the win-probability-added in those games, shrunk towards zero so a few lucky games can't top the list. An option needs 5 games and the matchup 15. Only items you can still buy count, and your own games never do.
- The Matchup card shows the same in champ select ("Did best in this matchup"). As before, nothing is written to the client unless you click, and only in champ select.

## Evaluation history
- The Data tab has a new **Evaluation history** card: every saved evaluation (yours and the automatic ones, the newest 30), with one chart for each question an evaluation answers:
  - draft-adjusted builds against the lane baseline;
  - patch memory against two patches;
  - runes against the draft against the overall win rate.
- **Each evaluation** is a dot with its 95 % interval, against a zero line:
  - **filled green dot:** better with 95 % confidence;
  - **hollow grey ring:** no clear difference;
  - **red triangle:** worse.

  Shapes and labels carry the verdict too, not only colours: green and red are hard to tell apart for colour-blind readers.
- **Each chart** says how often the option was better in the last 10 evaluations and how long the newest verdict has held. Hover or tab to a dot for its interval, date, games and reason; **As a table** lists every number.

## Champ select at 55,000 games
- **Checked at your size:** on a 54,000-game test database, every draft update in champ select took 1–3 seconds. The app re-weighed every stored game and rebuilt each decision's index on every update, though both only change with the model.
- **Now:** they are kept between updates. The first draft of a champ select takes about a second, and the next ones 12–34 ms, with exactly the same builds (tested). The kept data (100–200 MB at that size) is released 3 minutes after the last draft.
- **Also measured, no change needed:**
  - "Next item" in game takes 8–31 ms per update (the target is under 50 ms).
  - A model rebuild takes 15–30 s in the background, never during champ select or a game.
  - The model kept between builds holds 123 MB of data (211 MB with patch memory).

## After installing
Nothing to do. **Runes against the draft** stays off until Evaluate builds finds it better on your games; the next evaluation (yours or the automatic one) tests it. The history fills from the evaluations you already have.
